What the system does, in one sentence

SafeRide watches traffic footage — uploaded videos, a webcam, or an RTSP camera — and automatically finds motorcycle riders without helmets, capturing an annotated evidence photo, the rider's license plate crop, and the OCR-read plate text for every violation, queued for human review.

Key design choice: the system is a review assistant, not a judge — every saved violation lands in a human review queue where an officer confirms it or marks it a false positive.

Everything runs on one machine. The frontend never touches the models — it only talks HTTP to the backend, so either side can be swapped independently.

The three models — each does one job

detector 1

Object model · YOLO11s

  • COCO-pretrained, untouched
  • Finds person, motorcycle + car/bus/truck as negative context
  • Anchors association & tracking
  • Inference at 960 px
detector 2 · fine-tuned

Helmet model · YOLOv8n

  • Two classes: With Helmet / Without Helmet
  • Fine-tuned on 325 labeled frames of our Thai footage (round-2 weights staged, promotion after A/B)
  • Runs on rider-focused crops, not the full frame
detector 3 · fine-tuned

Plate model · YOLO11n

  • One class: License_Plate
  • Fine-tuned on Thai near-square motorcycle plates (round-2 weights staged)
  • Crops feed EasyOCR (Thai + English) for plate text

Device is auto-selected: CUDA with FP16 on the RTX 4070 SUPER (falls back to Apple MPS or CPU). Models load once and stay in memory; a full 3-model pass takes well under 200 ms on GPU.

What happens to every sampled frame

Sample One frame per second is fully analyzed (wall-clock for live, frame-count for files). Skipped frames are only decoded when someone is watching the stream.

Detect objects YOLO11s finds people, motorcycles, and cars/buses/trucks (the latter only as "not a motorcycle" context for plate assignment).

Detect helmets on crops A region is built above each motorcycle (heads sit ~1 bike-height above the box top), merged with overlapping person boxes, and the helmet model runs on those crops — several times more pixels on distant heads than a full-frame pass.

Detect plates + associate The plate model runs full-frame; then geometry scoring links helmet→person→motorcycle→plate into per-rider associations.

Track & vote ByteTrack keeps a stable ID per motorcycle across frames; each association casts a helmet / no-helmet vote for its track.

Confirm & save Tracks that pass the vote gate wait ~2 s collecting plate crops, then save one violation: annotated evidence JPEG, best plate crop, OCR text, DB record.

Adaptive burst: the moment any no-helmet box appears, sampling density jumps 5× (every 0.2 s) for 2.5 s — short-lived riders still accumulate enough votes.

Association — linking a head to a bike to a plate

A no-helmet head alone is not a violation. It must belong to a person on a motorcycle — pure geometry scoring, no extra ML:

Helmet → person

The helmet box must sit in the person's upper 62% ("head zone"). Score = in-zone bonus + overlap + distance to head position. Assignment is greedy one-to-one, so on a two-up bike the passenger can't steal the driver's helmet.

Person → motorcycle

The person's lower body (hips down) must sit inside/near the motorcycle box. Without a plausible motorcycle, a no-helmet person is discarded — that's what stops pedestrians from becoming violations.

Plate → motorcycle

Hard gates first: plate center in the bike's lower region, sane area ratio, and near-square aspect (Thai bike plates ≈ square, car plates are wide). If a nearby car/bus/truck fits the plate better, the bike loses it. One-to-one: two riders can never claim the same plate.

# final association score (0–1), gated at ≥ 0.38 for no-helmet saves score = 0.30·helmet_confidence + 0.30·helmet↔person + 0.25·person↔motorcycle + 0.15·plate↔motorcycle

Tracking & voting — why one bad frame can't create a violation

identity

ByteTrack on motorcycle boxes

  • Motorcycle detections are the most stable signal, so tracks anchor to bikes, not heads or people.
  • Two-pass matching: high-confidence boxes match first, low-confidence ones rescue occluded tracks.
  • Motion (IoU) blended with an HSV color histogram appearance feature — identity survives the 1 s gaps between samples.
  • A track survives ~3 s of being lost before it's dropped.
confirmation

Helmet voting per track

  • Every sampled frame, each rider association casts a helmet or no-helmet vote for its track.
  • Save requires ≥ 2 no-helmet votes, and no-helmet×2 ≥ with-helmet — a single model flicker never fires.
  • A genuinely mixed track (helmeted driver, bare-headed passenger) still reaches human review.
  • 4 s cooldown per track + spatial dedup (same bike location under a churned track ID is suppressed).

Before saving, a violation waits ~2 s collecting plate crops. Each crop is scored by detector confidence, size, sharpness, and OCR result; the best one wins, and OCR reads across the window are voted into the final plate text. A plate must also co-travel with the track across samples — a passing car's plate is dropped, not attached.

How the boxes get drawn

backend · OpenCV

Annotated frames (live stream + evidence)

For the MJPEG live stream and every evidence JPEG, the backend draws with OpenCV directly on the frame: one colored rectangle + label per detection, a status banner on top (SCANNING / red VIOLATION), and for each no-helmet rider an association line: white dot on the head → white line to the motorcycle center → yellow line to the plate, plus the track ID. Reviewers can see exactly which helmet, bike, and plate were linked.

frontend · canvas

Replay overlays (uploaded video playback)

For playback, the browser plays the original untouched video in a <video> tag. The backend writes per-sampled-frame detection metadata (boxes + timestamps) to JSON; the frontend fetches it and draws boxes on a <canvas> synchronized to playback time, scaling coordinates from source resolution to the displayed size. Boxes update at sampling cadence — the video itself never has burned-in graphics.

Backend & frontend — how the app hangs together

FastAPI backend

  • POST /api/videos/upload and POST /api/live/start create a job and kick off a background task — the API stays responsive while analysis runs.
  • GET /api/jobs/{id} — progress, frame counts, FPS, ETA, violation count.
  • GET /api/jobs/{id}/stream — MJPEG (multipart/x-mixed-replace), annotated frames at up to 12 fps, only rendered while someone is watching.
  • GET /api/jobs/{id}/detections — per-frame box metadata for canvas overlays.
  • GET/PATCH /api/settings — runtime tuning of confidences, sample rate, OCR.
  • PATCH /api/violations/{id}/review — confirm / false-positive decisions.

Next.js frontend

  • Analysis Console (/upload) — drag & drop upload, playback with overlays, telemetry, settings panel.
  • Live Monitor (/live) — webcam index or RTSP URL, MJPEG viewer, live evidence sidebar.
  • Dashboard (/dashboard) — job history, search, reopen replays.
  • Review Queue (/violations) — evidence inspector, plate crops, review decisions, CSV export.
  • Simple polling model: job status every 1.5 s while processing — no WebSockets needed at this scale.

Live mode — same brain, different input

input

Webcam or RTSP

OpenCV opens device index 0 (DirectShow) or any rtsp:// URL with a 1-frame buffer so analysis stays near-live. Credentials are stripped from saved job names.

identical pipeline

Zero duplicated logic

Live sessions call the exact same detection, association, tracking, and violation functions as uploads. Only sampling differs: wall-clock timed instead of frame-counted, since a camera delivers frames at its own pace.

safety rails

Bounded sessions

Every frame is recorded to MP4 for replay. A session ends on operator stop, source loss (~3 s of failed reads), the 25-violation cap, or a 15-minute safety limit.

Because live recordings are saved as normal jobs, a live session is replayable and reviewable afterwards exactly like an uploaded video — same evidence flow, same review queue.

Accuracy — measured, not guessed

We labeled 325 frames of our own Thai traffic footage (Label Studio, 260 train / 65 held-out val) and fine-tuned the two specialist models. Both old and new weights were validated on the identical 65 held-out images:

What the numbers mean (and don't)

  • mAP50: detection quality where a box counts as correct at ≥50% overlap with ground truth — the standard detector metric.
  • These are detector metrics, not end-to-end violation accuracy — voting and association filter further downstream.
  • Plate OCR reads ~70% in rough testing; motorcycle plates are harder than car plates (smaller, blurrier, more tilted).
  • Val set is our own footage = the deployment distribution; night, rain, and new angles remain unproven.

Honest limitations — say them before they're asked

detection

Sampled, not every frame

Analysis runs ~1×/s (5×/s in bursts). A rider crossing the frame in under a second could be missed. Trade-off is deliberate: real-time throughput on one GPU.

conditions

Trained on our footage

Night, heavy rain, and camera angles absent from the 325 labeled frames are unproven. Detector metrics also aren't end-to-end violation-event metrics — that A/B evaluation is the next step.

scope

MVP boundaries

No authentication or roles yet. Runtime settings reset on restart. OCR misses small or blurred bike plates. Association is geometry-based and tuned by hand, not learned.

Mitigations already built in: every violation carries visual evidence with the association drawn on it, and a human makes the final call — the system's errors are reviewable, never silent.

Q&A cheat sheet — design questions

Why three separate models instead of one?
Each solves a different problem at a different scale. COCO YOLO11s is already excellent at people/vehicles — no data needed. Helmets and Thai plates need specialist training. Separate models can be fine-tuned, swapped, and evaluated independently — we replaced two of them without touching a line of pipeline code.
Why track motorcycles instead of people or heads?
Motorcycle boxes are the most stable detection across frames — heads are tiny and people overlap each other in traffic. Rider identity is anchored to the bike, and helmet observations vote onto that track.
How do you avoid false violations?
Five stacked gates: confidence thresholds → geometric association (a no-helmet head must belong to a person on a motorcycle, score ≥ 0.38) → ≥2 no-helmet votes per track with votes not drowned out by with-helmet votes → per-track cooldown + spatial dedup → human review as the final decision.
What stops a car's plate being attached to a rider?
Three defenses: Thai bike plates are near-square while car plates are wide (aspect gate); car/bus/truck detections compete for the plate and win if they fit better; and the plate must co-travel with the rider's track across multiple samples.
What about a passenger without a helmet when the driver has one?
Helmet-to-person assignment is one-to-one, so the passenger gets their own no-helmet observation, and the mixed track still passes the vote gate (no-helmet×2 ≥ with-helmet) into human review.

Q&A cheat sheet — practical questions

Is it real-time?
Yes for the video feed; detection boxes refresh once per second (five per second near a suspected violation). A full 3-model pass takes under 200 ms on the GPU, so the cadence is a design choice for headroom, not a hardware limit — it's one config value.
What hardware does it need?
Developed on an RTX 4070 SUPER with CUDA FP16. The device is auto-selected — it also runs on Apple Silicon (MPS) or CPU, just with slower sampling. Nothing requires the cloud.
How accurate is it really?
Fine-tuned detectors: helmet mAP50 0.86, plate 0.94 on 65 held-out frames of our footage — up from 0.39 and 0.56 for the public baselines. OCR reads ~70% of plates. End-to-end violation-event accuracy is the next measurement, via a labeled-clip A/B script.
What about privacy?
Everything is processed and stored locally — no cloud upload, no third-party APIs. Only flagged violation frames are kept as evidence; RTSP credentials are stripped from job names; records can be deleted from the UI.
What would you do next?
1) End-to-end A/B evaluation to promote the round-2 weights with confidence. 2) Label night/rain/new-angle footage — the known blind spots. 3) Thai-plate-specific OCR post-processing. 4) Auth + persisted settings for a real deployment, and PDF violation reports.
