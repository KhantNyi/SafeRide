Why this matters

Helmets save lives. Enforcement doesn't scale.

the risk

Motorcyclists carry the burden

Thailand has one of the world's highest road-fatality rates, and roughly three in four of those deaths are riders of two- and three-wheelers. A helmet is the single best predictor of surviving a crash.

the gap

Manual enforcement is spot checks

Checkpoints see a fraction of traffic, at fixed places and times. Hours of camera footage exist — but nobody can watch it all, frame by frame.

the idea

Let the model watch, let humans decide

SafeRide turns raw footage into a short review queue: who rode without a helmet, when, on which motorcycle, with the plate captured — pending a human confirm.

The core

Detection pipeline, per sampled frame

  1. Sample smart. Analyze ~1 frame/second; skipped frames are advanced without decoding. The moment a no-helmet detection appears, sampling densifies 5× for 2.5 s (adaptive sampling).
  2. Detect in three passes. A general detector finds people, motorcycles, and car/bus/truck context on the full frame. The helmet model runs on rider-focused crops around each motorcycle — several times more pixels on distant heads. The plate detector scans the full frame.
  3. Associate with geometry. Helmet boxes are assigned one-to-one to people (drivers and passengers); plates one-to-one to motorcycles; people to motorcycles by position — every link gated by a minimum score.
  4. Track identity. A ByteTrack-style tracker follows raw motorcycle boxes across samples; helmet observations accumulate as votes per track.
  5. Save deliberately. A violation is written only after enough no-helmet votes, a cooldown, plate co-travel checks, and spatial duplicate suppression — one record per rider, with the best plate crop from the whole track.

Precision engineering

Every gate kills a specific failure mode

helmet votes ≥ 2

A single-frame model flicker on a helmeted rider can't create evidence. Mixed driver/passenger tracks still pass to human review.

1:1 helmet ↔ person

On two-up bikes the driver and passenger each get their own helmet observation — an unhelmeted passenger behind a helmeted driver is caught.

1:1 plate ↔ motorcycle

Two riders can never claim the same plate; ambiguous plates (two candidate bikes within a margin) are dropped for that frame.

plate co-travel

A plate must appear with the rider's track across ≥2 samples — a passing car's plate seen once is never attached.

adaptive sampling 5×

Dense sampling around candidate violations gives short-lived and fast-moving riders enough observations to qualify — cost stays local to the event.

spatial dedup

A save within one bike-size of a recent save is the same rider, whatever the track id says — tracker churn can't double-ticket anyone.

Plate reading

Thai plates are multi-line — read them that way

  1. Best crop wins. Over a 2 s window the pipeline keeps the sharpest, largest, highest-confidence plate crop for the track — not the first one.
  2. Three preprocess variants (raw, upscaled, adaptive-threshold) are OCR'd; lines are classified by shape — registration prefix 1กข, digit group 1234, province เชียงใหม่ — and recombined top-to-bottom.
  3. Pattern beats confidence. A plate-shaped reading outranks high-confidence noise; one-character junk is rejected outright.
  4. Votes across time. The text read consistently across a track's samples beats a single lucky read.

Display rules

Accuracy matrix

Two instruments, five metrics

offline · evaluate.py

Labeled-clip eval harness

Runs real clips with hand-labeled violation frame ranges through the actual pipeline, then scores:

  • Precision — saved records that match a real event
  • Recall — real events that produced a record
  • Duplicate rate — extra records per detected rider
  • Plate capture rate — matched events with a crop
  • OCR match rate — plates read exactly right
online · /api/metrics/review

Human-review precision

Every confirm / false-positive decision in the review queue feeds live precision metrics — overall, per job, and per helmet-confidence band.

Current standing from 16 human decisions: 68.8% precision (11 confirmed, 5 false positives) — measured on records from the previous pipeline version.

Same principle in both: never tune a threshold you can't measure.

Analysis

What the numbers say

design bias: precision

False positives are the enemy

Every saved record costs reviewer time and credibility. The vote gate, association gates, and dedup all trade a little recall for a much cleaner queue — the right trade for an enforcement-review tool.

where errors come from

Three dominant sources

  • Helmet baseline model not trained on Thai road scenes
  • Motion blur + distance degrade plate crops before OCR ever runs
  • Dense, occluded traffic strains the geometric association
feedback loop

Review decisions are training data

Confirmed and false-positive evidence frames are exactly the hard examples a fine-tuned helmet model needs — the review queue quietly builds the next dataset.

Honest constraints

Known limitations

model
  • Helmet detector is a public baseline — domain gap on Thai footage
  • OCR is general-purpose; full-plate exact reads are still rare
  • A rider must appear in ≥2 sampled frames to be saved
pipeline
  • Sampled analysis can miss events between samples (adaptive mode narrows, doesn't close, this gap)
  • Identity anchors to motorcycle tracks — heavy occlusion can still split a rider across ids
  • CPU-only inference in the current deployment
system
  • Background tasks aren't durable across a backend restart
  • SQLite + local disk: fine locally, not multi-user production
  • No authentication or media access control yet
measurement
  • No frame-accurate labeled ground truth yet — smoke metrics are indicative
  • Review-precision metric spans mixed pipeline versions
